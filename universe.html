    Every event has one of four verbs: travel (something moved between planets: a comet), dock (a standing relationship: a dart into orbit), place (something on or off a book: a rock in a belt), happen (the account itself changed: a ring of light); a failed transaction is a spark that never leaves its planet, a Batch a tick. Every body is earned by data: a planet is an account that appeared in a validated transaction the page heard; a star issues a currency to at least three holders in those records; a black hole candidate has its master key disabled and an unusable regular key in its account root; a pulsar has a measured cadence. Every body's label prints the threshold that made it. A planet is named by its on-ledger Domain, else by the label the listening page's cast gives it (a cast member wears a ring in its cast colour), else by its short address. The coverage line says how much was heard. Nothing is stored; reloading starts an empty sky.

    The UFO is the probe’s cursor over the sky: double right-click the sky, press U, or double-tap with two fingers to put it away or bring it back. Hovering a planet with it shows a soft beam; holding (the button, a touch, or Space on a focused planet) locks the beam and probes the account: its recent history through the node with the ledgers stated, the planets the record connects it to lit with their counts, and four measures with their labels in the readout’s own words: record proximity (a random walk with restart over the window’s touch graph), flow conservation (exact inflow and outflow per currency and the lag at which they match), its own cadence, and coherence against its counterparties and the nearest active planets (the prewhitened statistic with its null, pairs tested and the percentile, and whether the record connects the pair). Releasing retracts the beam; the catch stays lit until the next hold or Esc. The beam pulls by evidence only: nothing in the sky moves toward it without a number. The probe also asks whether the account and the makers on its books share a price: each account’s funded offers are the prices under which they all rest, and a group shares a price when one price fits them all (the nerve of market cones after G. Chichilnisky, Bull. AMS 29 (1993) and J. Math. Econ. 28 (1997), of which only the nerve, Helly’s theorem and LP duality are used; the equilibrium theorem is contested and unused); when no price fits, the readout shows the loop of offers, its edge after the engine’s transfer fees, and its room, at the ledger it was read.

    With prefers-reduced-motion the sky stands still: no slow turn, comets as still lines that fade, a beam that is on or off, a saucer that does not bob.
